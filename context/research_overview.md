# Research Overview

## Research area

Effective-field-theory approaches to quantum gravity, with emphasis on
low-energy predictions that can be extracted without knowing the ultraviolet
completion of quantum gravity.

## Main research direction

The current short-term focus (2026-09-18) is how the EFT interpretation and
explicit UV matching restrict higher-derivative gravity's solutions and
initial data. The proposed route is a solvable light/heavy toy model, followed
by quadratic gravity as a baseline and a possible Riemann-cubed or
matter-coupled application. See `current_state.md` and the linked vault review.

The earlier investigation concerns corrections to classical gravitational
observables derived from quantum field theory and effective general relativity.

A central example is the long-distance correction to the Newtonian potential.

That thread's conceptual starting point is understanding the structure and
derivation of the one-loop correction discussed in Donoghue's treatment of
effective quantum gravity.

## Main questions

Current broad questions include:

- How are classical and quantum contributions distributed between tree and
  loop diagrams in gravity?
- Why can loop diagrams contain classical contributions?
- Which nonanalytic terms in scattering amplitudes generate long-range
  corrections to potentials?
- How do additional operators in the gravitational EFT Lagrangian affect
  observable quantities?
- In particular, how can derivative and curvature operators modify one-loop
  corrections to the gravitational potential?
- Which corrections are universal low-energy predictions and which depend on
  unknown EFT coefficients?

## Computational approach

The project combines analytical understanding with symbolic computation.

Primary tools currently include:

- Mathematica / Wolfram Language;
- FeynCalc;
- FeynGrav;
- Python where useful for numerical work, plotting, or independent checks.

Important calculations should be made reproducible through textual source files
and version control.
